Startup Legal Checklist for First-Time Founders

The short answer: form the company cleanly, assign the IP, document founder equity, keep securities records, use real contracts, and build a tidy data room before anyone important asks for it.

Published January 18, 2024 by Alex Ravski · Updated August 1, 2026

What should founders handle first?

Most startup legal problems are not cinematic. They are small skipped steps that become expensive at exactly the wrong time. A missing contractor IP assignment is boring until a customer, investor, or acquirer asks who owns the code. Then it is suddenly the main character.

For a first-time founder, the goal is not to lawyer every decision into paste. The goal is to make sure the company can raise money, hire, sell, and survive diligence without unnecessary drama.

The founder legal checklist

  • Choose the right entity. Venture-backed startups usually use a Delaware C-corporation, but tax, geography, and business model can affect the answer.
  • Issue founder stock correctly. Board approvals, stock purchase agreements, vesting, and 83(b) elections should be handled early.
  • Assign IP to the company. Every founder, contractor, employee, and advisor who touches product should sign the right invention assignment paperwork.
  • Approve equity grants properly. Options, advisor shares, and consultant equity need board approval and clean records.
  • Keep fundraising records. SAFEs, notes, side letters, wire confirmations, and investor communications should be organized from day one.
  • Use customer and vendor contracts that match the business. A SaaS company, AI company, marketplace, and hardware company do not have the same risk profile.
  • Build a data room before diligence. Corporate charter, bylaws, board consents, cap table, IP agreements, employee documents, and material contracts should be easy to find.
  • Check beneficial ownership reporting. Corporate Transparency Act (BOI) reporting now applies mainly to foreign reporting companies following FinCEN's March 2025 rule change; confirm whether it still touches your entity.

When does this become urgent?

It becomes urgent before a financing, before an enterprise contract, before a co-founder leaves, before hiring employees, and before any M&A conversation. In other words, usually before founders feel like they have time for it.

FAQ

Do I need a lawyer before I have revenue?

Often, yes. Formation, IP ownership, equity, and fundraising documents are cheaper to set up cleanly than to repair after the company has momentum.

Can I use online templates?

Templates can help with simple starts, but founders should be careful when issuing equity, raising money, assigning IP, or signing strategic contracts. Those are not great places for copy-and-pray legal work.
